Since 1942

Our history

How Round Tablers built a network of Masonic lodges

Round Table was built for young men, and it has always had an upper age limit. As Tablers reached it, many looked for a way to keep the friendships and the spirit of service going. Some joined 41 Club; others, who were also Freemasons, started lodges of their own. Over eighty years those lodges have grown into a network across England and Wales and beyond, meeting every two years at the Biennial Reunion.

This page is a first draft put together from lodge and provincial websites, published lodge records and Reunion papers. Dates are given where a source states them. If you can correct or add to it, please get in touch.

Tap a dot to jump to that moment.

Beginnings

  1. 1927
    Round Table

    Round Table is founded in Norwich

    Louis Marchesi starts the first Round Table in Norwich as a club where young business and professional men can meet, make friends and serve their community. The movement spreads quickly across Britain and abroad.

  2. 1942
    Lodges

    Astolat Lodge, Guildford

    In the middle of the Second World War, a group of Guildford Freemasons plan a new lodge and decide that its first members should come, as far as possible, from past and present Round Tablers. It takes its name from Astolat, the Arthurian name for Guildford. Warranted in April and consecrated in May 1942, it is the earliest lodge with Round Table roots that we know of.

    Astolat Lodge No. 5848 · Surrey

  3. 1951–52
    Lodges

    Lodge of Harmony, Bristol

    Bristol’s Round Tables ask for a lodge of their own. Lodge of Harmony is established in 1951 and consecrated in January 1952, still meeting today at Freemasons’ Hall in Park Street.

    Lodge of Harmony No. 7127 · Bristol

  4. 1958
    Lodges

    The Round Table name arrives

    Round Table Lodge in London is warranted, the earliest lodge we have found that carries the Round Table name itself. In the years that follow, lodges of the Round Table are formed in Leicester, Worcestershire and Sussex.

    Round Table Lodge No. 7619 · Lodge of the Round Table No. 7762 · Lodge of the Round Table No. 7961 · Round Table Lodge of Sussex No. 7965

Growth

From the mid-1960s, new lodges appear across the country, usually founded by members of the local Round Tables and often taking the name of their county.

  1. 1966–69
    Lodges

    Lancashire, Essex, Lincolnshire, Staffordshire and Middlesex

    Round Table Lodge at Cleveleys is warranted in 1966. In 1968 the Round Table Lodges of Essex (Hutton) and Lincolnshire (Horncastle) follow, the latter founded by former Tablers wanting to combine Round Table camaraderie with Freemasonry. Lodges at Wednesbury and in Middlesex follow in 1969.

    Nos. 8111, 8217, 8240, 8273 · Round Table Lodge of Middlesex No. 8301

  2. 1971
    Lodges

    North Staffordshire

    North Staffordshire Round Table Lodge is founded in Stoke-on-Trent; it marked its fiftieth anniversary in 2021.

    North Staffordshire Round Table Lodge No. 8355

  3. 1970s
    Lodges

    Warwickshire, Norfolk, Nottinghamshire, Kent, Derbyshire and Cheshire

    The Lodge of Round Table in Warwickshire is followed by the Round Table Lodges of Norfolk and Nottinghamshire (both 1974). In 1976 Kentish Round Table Lodge is formed by members of Round Tables in West Kent, and the Round Table Lodge of Derbyshire by ex-Tablers looking to carry on their purpose. The Round Table Lodge of South Cheshire follows in 1977.

    Nos. 8424, 8600, 8604, 8685, 8725, 8787

The 1980s

  1. 1981–83
    Lodges

    Surrey, Gloucestershire, South Derbyshire and Somerset

    Table Fellowship Lodge is consecrated in November 1981 by Surrey Freemasons and Round Tablers. Gloucestershire Round Table Lodge is consecrated in January 1982. In 1983 Burton-upon-Trent Tablers found the Round Table Lodge of South Derbyshire, and Somerset Tablers Lodge is founded by members of Round Table Area 37, which then covered the whole county.

    Nos. 8989, 9017, 9074, 9075

  2. 1984–88
    Lodges

    Shropshire, Hertfordshire, Suffolk, Essex and Yorkshire

    Shropshire Round Table Lodge is followed by the Round Table Lodge of Hertfordshire, consecrated in St Albans in April 1987, and in 1988 by the Round Table Lodge of Suffolk and West Essex Round Table Lodge. The Lodge of King Arthur, whose members come from the Yorkshire and Lancashire Tables, also dates from this period.

    Nos. 9104, 9221, 9276, 9310, 9311

The 1990s

  1. 1993
    Lodges

    Oxfordshire

    The Round Table Lodge of Oxfordshire is warranted.

    Round Table Lodge of Oxfordshire No. 9509

  2. 1994
    Lodges

    Cardiff and North Essex

    The Lodge of Round Table is founded in Cardiff, bringing the network to Wales. Louis Marchesi Lodge in Devon, named after the founder of Round Table, follows around the same time, and in December North Essex Round Table Lodge is warranted at Clacton-on-Sea.

    Lodge of Round Table No. 9549 · Louis Marchesi Lodge No. 9558 · North Essex Round Table Lodge No. 9578

  3. 1990s
    Lodges

    Ridings Tablers, Beverley

    Ridings Tablers Lodge brings the network to East Yorkshire.

    Ridings Tablers Lodge No. 9586

  4. 1999
    Lodges

    Thames Valley and Nairobi

    Round Table Thames Valley Lodge is established at Henley-on-Thames as a home for Tablers, ex-Tablers and those who ‘should have been Tablers’. The same year a Round Table Lodge is warranted in Nairobi, Kenya.

    Round Table Thames Valley Lodge No. 9697 · Round Table Lodge No. 9711

Today

  1. Now
    Round Table

    Almost fifty lodges

    There are now almost fifty Round Table lodges in England and Wales. Many no longer recruit only from Round Table, but they keep its traditions: fun and fellowship alongside service to the community, and a warm welcome for visitors. Every two years the lodges come together for the Biennial Reunion, a tradition that goes back many years, hosted in turn by one of the lodges.

    See the directory of lodges

  2. 2022
    Reunion

    Biennial Reunion at Crewe Hall

    North Staffordshire Round Table Lodge hosts the Round Table Lodges Biennial weekend at Crewe Hall, 10–12 June 2022.

    Hosted by No. 8355

  3. 2024
    Reunion

    Biennial Reunion in Torquay

    Louis Marchesi Lodge takes the Reunion to the seaside at the Anchorage Hotel in Babbacombe, 10–12 May 2024: a pre-tour for early arrivals, a Lodge meeting at St Marychurch Masonic Hall, a ladies’ dinner hosted by former members of Ladies Circle, beach games and a fish and chip lunch at Paignton, and a gala dinner to finish.

    Hosted by No. 9558

  4. 2026
    Reunion

    Biennial Reunion in Cardiff

    The Lodge of Round Table hosts the Biennial Reunion in Cardiff, 15–17 May 2026: a Lodge Installation Meeting at Cardiff Masonic Hall, a private tour of Cardiff Castle and a black-tie gala dinner at Cornerstone.

    Cardiff 2026 · Hosted by No. 9549
